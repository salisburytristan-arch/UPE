# Purchase Options

This document outlines the main commercial paths available for evaluating and adopting **Unified Physics Engine (UPE)**.

> Final rights, pricing, support, warranties, and exclusivity are defined only in signed transaction documents.

## 1) Pilot / Integration License

**Best for:** teams that want to validate UPE in a real build before a larger commitment.

Typical use:
- short-term technical evaluation in a product branch
- integration spike for rollback, replay, or headless simulation
- conversion credit toward a broader commercial deal

## 2) Internal-Use Source License

**Best for:** companies using UPE inside internal tools, simulators, QA systems, or proprietary workflows.

Typical use:
- digital-twin or simulation tooling
- internal prototyping and validation
- non-redistributed, source-level customization

## 3) Enterprise Source License

**Best for:** studios and platform teams that need broader modification rights and a longer-term integration path.

Typical use:
- engine-level embedding
- proprietary shipping products
- negotiated maintenance, update, or support terms

## 4) Field / Vertical-Exclusive License

**Best for:** buyers who want exclusivity inside a defined market, platform, or application area.

Typical use:
- exclusive rights for a specific vertical
- platform- or market-limited exclusivity
- strategic differentiation without full acquisition

## 5) Full Acquisition / IP Transfer

**Best for:** buyers seeking outright ownership of the asset.

Typical use:
- full buyout with negotiated transfer scope
- assignment of IP under a separate signed agreement
- premium transaction for long-term strategic control

## Why This Matters

Multiple purchase paths make UPE easier to adopt because buyers do not have to say yes to a full acquisition on day one. They can choose a transaction structure that matches their risk tolerance, budget, and intended use.
